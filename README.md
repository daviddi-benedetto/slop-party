# Slop Party

*(formerly Slop Game, and before that Fragdeck. Old settings files carry over.)*

Local splitscreen first-person shooter for 1–6 players on one PC, with five
game modes:

- **Rounds** (default) – free-for-all deathmatch. Every time you die you are
  dealt three random powerup cards and keep one for the rest of the match, so
  whoever is losing keeps getting stronger. First to the kill target wins.
- **Zombies** – co-op survival. Zombies follow you anywhere: they climb
  walls and scramble onto roofs, so there is nowhere safe to camp. Your squad (plus optional CPU allies) holds out
  against endless, ever-growing waves of walkers, runners, brutes and a boss
  every fifth wave. Everyone picks a powerup between waves; fallen players
  return when the wave is cleared. It lasts until the whole squad is down.
- **Frontline** – Battlefield-inspired team war on a huge 200 × 140 m map:
  blue vs orange (humans split between the teams, or all together against
  the CPU), conquest flags A/B/C, tickets, parachute drops, and vehicles –
  buggies, tanks and helicopters. Two maps: **Frontline** and **Hightown**
  (pick with the MAP chip, RB / M).
- **Racing** – toy Formula cars on the **Raceway**, a circuit with kerbs,
  crash barriers, a grandstand and a chequered start under a gantry. Pick
  the laps (2–12, X / K); CPU racers fill the grid to six. Y / E puts your
  car back on the track if you spin off. Race in third or first person (your
  animal's paws on the steering wheel).
  Party-kart extras: drive through the **gift boxes** for an item (RT /
  click to use): **Turbo**, **Triple Turbo**, **Bonk Bomb** (rolls down the
  track and spins out the first car it reaches), **Goo** (dropped behind
  you) and **Bubble Shield** (blocks one hit). Two **perks** last the whole
  race: **Sticky Tyres** (sharper steering) and **Big Tank** (longer
  turbos). Cars further back get better items. **Boost pads** on the
  straights; **coins** add top speed (up to 10, you drop 2 when spun out).
  A little village fills the infield.
- **Gun Game** – free-for-all where every KO hands you the next weapon on a
  ladder of all 31 (guns, then magic, then medieval arms, ending on the
  Sword). Being finished off with a melee knife knocks you one rung down. First to
  score with the last weapon wins. No cards, quick respawns.
- **Medieval** – a free-for-all in the **Castle** with medieval arms only:
  Sword, Battle Axe, Spear, Longbow, Crossbow, Throwing Knives and Powder
  Bombs. Your block is a shield (it lasts longer and returns sooner), and
  cards and mystery boxes only deal medieval weapons.

You play as one of seven plush animals in split screen, on seven arenas plus
a zombie campus and a huge battlefield, in a visual theme of your choice.
Everything you need in a match is also in the game: **Options > HOW TO
PLAY** lists every button and **MODES** explains each mode.

### Characters

- **Seven plush animals:** Bunny, Kitty, Pup, Bear, Fox, Panda and Duck, made
  for this game: big heads, soft fuzzy bodies, painted patches, glossy
  button eyes that blink, wagging tails and stubby paws that hold the weapon.
- **Customise** on your lobby card: animal, colour (your bandana), hat and
  back item, all remembered between sessions. Each player's own look speed
  is in **Options > Controls** (Player 1-6 look speed).
- **Body language and faces:** animals lean into turns, tilt into the
  wall on a wall run, sit back low with feet first in a slide, snap away
  from hits and fidget when standing still. Eyes squint when hit, go wide
  when health is low and smile after a KO; the mouth opens when hit or
  taunting.
- **Taunts:** Wave, Dance, Nope and Flex on the D-pad (keys 1–4); your camera
  pulls out so you can watch yourself.

### Weapons, cards and combat

- **31 weapons:** 20 guns (Blaster, Scatter, Zapper, Boomer, Rail, Lobber,
  Bubbler, Tripler, Magnum, Spinner, Pinball, Flamer, Tesla, Cluster, Vortex,
  Ripper, Sticky, Longshot, Frostbite and the akimbo Twins), 4 magic weapons
  (Fire Hands, Frost Hands, Storm Staff, Arcane Staff) and 7 medieval ones.
  Get them from weapon cards or from **mystery boxes**: open one (Y / E),
  the lid flips back, weapons spin up out of it, and the last one flies to
  you. Boxes are everywhere (7 on the smallest map, 26 on Hightown) and
  reopen 10 s after use, so you're never stuck with a weapon you don't like.
- **77 powerup cards** in three rarities (common, rare, epic): scopes and a
  red-dot sight, a jetpack, block perks that turn your block into a weapon
  (Blink Guard, Frost Guard, Radiant Guard...), eight melee upgrades, and
  oddities like Tiny, Giant, Goomba Stomp, Buddy Drone and Chain Reaction.
  Every on-hit card works with every weapon, melee and magic included.
- **Movement inspired by Titanfall:** sprint, crouch, slide, wall run, wall
  jump, mantle, dash and aim down sights.
- **Block** reflects every bullet, rocket, grenade and flame back at whoever
  fired it. In first person your weapon floats on its own (no arms in the
  way); magic is cast from open
  mitten paws with toe beans. Swords, axes, spears and staffs sit out at
  the side of the screen so you can see. **Melee** with any gun in hand: a
  floating knife slashes (wind-up, fast cut, a jolt on impact). Swords turn
  edge-first into every slash; axes and spears swing the blade itself.
- **Clear feedback:** hit markers (gold for headshots), a red cross and a
  stamped "FRAGGED!" on a knockout (DOUBLE KO / TRIPLE KO for quick
  chains), arcs showing where damage came from, a view flinch away from
  the hit, a pulsing red edge when your health is low, and on death your
  view turns to face whoever got you and shows how much health they had
  left. Bullets leave holes and puffs of dust; blasts leave scorch marks
  and smoke.
- **Gore, in every mode:** every hit sprays blood that splatters the floor
  and the wall behind the victim. A KO paints the area around the body and
  sends parts flying: meat, bones, guts, severed limbs and the head. A
  headshot bursts the head (skull chunks, eyes, brains), a blast blows the
  body apart, and a melee KO lops off a limb. Splatter stays for about a
  minute, so a long fight (or a zombie wave) turns into a real bloodbath.
  The bullet hole / scorch mark option also turns the splatter off.

### HUD and menus

- **The HUD speaks the theme:** highlights, panels and accents take the
  theme's colours (pink and cyan in Cyberpunk, pastel in Toy...), and so do
  the pause menu, options and results.
- **Weapon silhouettes** instead of names: on your weapon badge, in the
  kill feed (what did it, including the knife), and when a mystery box
  hands you something.
- **Health in chunks of 25** that shakes when you are hit, drains to show
  what you lost, glows green when you heal and pulses red near death.
- **Clean, modern HUD:** a thin light-grey ring for a
  crosshair (no centre dot) that widens with spread, slim bars and
  translucent panels with soft shadows instead of thick outlines; every
  edge is anti-aliased.
- **Reload ring** around the crosshair, a red tick when the magazine runs
  low, and the last bullets in the magazine shown red.
- **Small views stay readable:** in a small cell (5–6 way split, or a small
  window) the HUD drops the words and makes numbers and icons bigger.
- **Numbers in a chunky display font** (Lilita One): ammo, health, scores,
  the countdown and the results.
- **PlayStation pads** show the shape buttons (cross, circle, square,
  triangle) and L1 / R2 names; Xbox-style pads keep A / B / X / Y.
- **Screen transitions:** a slanted curtain in the theme's colours sweeps
  across between the lobby, the loading card, the match and the results.
- **Pause menu** pops in over the blurred match with the mode, map and the
  standings at a glance.
- **Results awards:** stats count up, and up to four titles are handed out
  (Up Close for melee KOs, Box Addict, On a Roll, Heavy Hitter, Survivor).

### Voxel world

The world is built from small cubes, in the spirit of voxel shooters like
Teardown: every wall, floor, building, tree, car and prop is made of 10 cm
voxels, each with its own flat colour taken from the original textures
(brick, plaster, timber...), with blocky silhouettes. Every face of every
voxel sits at its own random depth (lit edges where it steps down, shaded
corners where it steps up), so surfaces have texture at any distance; up
close the floors and walls really step in and out. The plush animals, their
guns (first and third person), vehicles, doors, mystery boxes, debris and
the grass (tufts of voxel blades) are voxels too; only the HUD, the sky and
effects are not. Nothing swaps detail by distance (no pop-in): the voxel
textures blend smoothly into the distance instead of shimmering. Options →
Graphics → **World style** switches back to the smooth look (from the next
match).

### Maps

- **Arenas** for Rounds and Gun Game: Neon Yard (container yard), Ember Pit
  (foundry), Frost Line (polar outpost), Museum (indoor: atrium, balconies,
  vaults, café, armoury hall), Stadium (pitch, bleachers, locker rooms, a
  club stand), Suburb (houses, yards, pools, a storm drain) and
  the Castle (walkable curtain walls, towers, gatehouses, a three-storey
  keep, a market, a siege camp; also Medieval mode's home).
- **Quarantine** (Zombies): a hospital campus with a clinic, triage tents, a
  warehouse, a motel, a tunnel and a morgue cellar.
- **Hightown:** a medieval city that grew upwards. Timber-and-plaster towers
  five to nine storeys tall around a market square, built from the
  Medieval Village MegaKit, with open windows to shoot and jump through,
  arcades, balconies, switchback stairs in every tower and roof terraces
  with railings at the top. Huge (200 × 140 m) for Frontline, and on the
  map list for Rounds and Gun Game (with unarmed rides, like Frontline).
- **Frontline:** a town with towers, warehouses, bunkers and trenches. Its
  own mode's home, and also on the lobby's map list for **Rounds** and
  **Gun Game**: a huge free-for-all with spawns spread over the town, roads,
  farms and flag yards. In Rounds there are **unarmed rides** for getting
  around: 2 buggies, 4 motorbikes and 2 helicopters. They have no guns and
  half the armour, so the fighting stays on foot, but running someone over
  still bumps them flying and hurts a bit (10 to 35 by speed, so three hits
  at most to KO). A
  destroyed ride comes back after 20 s.
- **Easy to move around:** doorways are open (no doors), windows are big
  enough to jump through (there is no glass anywhere), and nothing is placed in
  a doorway, on or at the foot of stairs, or in front of a window. Streets
  have no loose clutter; outdoor dressing stays put, and the small stuff
  does not block you. A few heavy things indoors barely move when you push
  them, and walking over anything never flings you into the air. Fuel barrels and red gas cans explode (indoors and out) and
  ruined walls crumble. Doorways, stairs and windows are always clear.
- **Scenery:** dense wind-blown cartoon grass (fat, round-tipped blades)
  and flowers on clumpy turf with bare patches, over rolling mounds, a
  horizon of hills and mountains on every map, warm lamps indoors, sun rays.
- **Surfaces with depth:** brick, stone, cobbles, tiles, planks, siding,
  shingles, metal panels and corrugated sheet have grooves you can see into
  (parallax occlusion mapping, option **Surface depth**): mortar lines and
  seams sink in, shift as you move and sit in their own shadow. Buildings
  have window jambs and roof cornices, and perimeter walls have pilasters,
  a coping, a skirting and fallen rubble at their foot.
- **Weathering:** walls are dirtier and damp at the foot and sun-bleached
  higher up, and big surfaces vary in colour so no two stretches match.
- **Wet neon nights:** Cyberpunk and Synthwave streets are rain-slick, with
  puddles that reflect the neon (option **Reflections on wet ground**).
  Spooky and Moonlit have a low mist pooling near the ground.
- **A world that moves:** trees, bushes and plants sway in the wind, and
  the tall towers on the skyline blink red warning lights.
- **Impacts that match the surface:** stone chips and dust, wood
  splinters, metal sparks with a ricochet ping, snow puffs, dirt and grass.
- **Reloads:** the floating gun dips and spins as it reloads.
- **Scopes really zoom:** the lens shows what is behind it magnified by the
  scope's power (2x, 4x).
- **Voxel gore:** blood flies as little cubes, splatter dries into 10 cm
  blocks, gibs are clumps of voxels, and zombies are voxel rigs that
  animate bone by bone.
- **Night and fog:** night themes have real distance fog (the far side of a
  map fades into the dark) and fewer glowing specks in the air; Zombies is
  wrapped in thick fog, so the horde comes out of the murk.
- **Lived-in world:** graffiti and posters on the perimeter walls, wind
  turbines turning on the distant hills and a blimp drifting round the
  arena (lit at night).
- **Screen effects** (option): a speed blur at the edges when you dash or
  slide, a zoom blur when a blast goes off nearby, and colour fringing when
  you are badly hurt.
- **Dramatic fire and smoke:** explosions throw a rolling fireball, streaks
  of glowing embers, a shock ring, a ring of dust along the ground and a
  column of black smoke that hangs for seconds; big blasts leave a fire
  burning. The Flamer spits real tongues of flame, rockets and fireballs
  leave smoke (and fire) trails, lava sheds embers, campfires burn, walls
  coming down throw up dust clouds, and muzzle flashes are hot stars.
- **Mystery boxes** float above their light rings, bobbing gently.
- **Hovering sights:** scopes and red-dot sights float above the gun on a
  glowing emitter and beam in your colour, bobbing gently (steady while you
  aim).
- **Guns and feet:** brass shell casings fly out of the guns that eject
  them, smoke hangs at the muzzle, paws leave prints that fade, and slides
  kick up dust.
- **Depth:** strong ambient occlusion (soft shadows in corners and under
  things, even in sunlight), so simple shapes and flat colours read in
  depth. (There are no ink outlines any more: they flickered.)
- **Light and air:** bounce light (colour from lit walls and floors spills
  into the shade), a reflection probe so glass, water and metal reflect the
  level, optional light shafts through the haze, and drifting specks in the
  air that suit the theme: pollen by day, embers at sunset, fireflies at
  night, neon dust in Cyberpunk and Synthwave, dust motes indoors. Neon
  lights buzz and stutter now and then, water ripples, and dirt patches and
  cracks break up big floors.

### Themes

One lobby setting (LB / T) restyles every map, the lobby's backyard and the
results screen:

- **Classic:** each map's own colours in clear daylight.
- **Toy:** pastel paint, bright soft daylight, gentle shadows.
- **Warm:** a relaxing golden hour, low amber sun, peach and lilac sky.
- **Cyberpunk:** every map at night with magenta haze and blooming pink and
  cyan neon.
- **Synthwave:** a retro pink sunset with teal and pink neon.
- **Spooky:** a Halloween night, pale moon, green haze, pumpkin lights.
- **Moonlit:** a calm clear night with each map's own lamps and stars.
- **Random:** a different theme each match.

Imported models get the same matte toy material and theme colours as
everything generated, so the world reads as one place. Weather stays clear;
large outdoor maps use a light distance haze.

Built with [Godot 4.7.2](https://godotengine.org) (MIT licensed), 100% GDScript.
The map layouts, textures, icons, characters and music are generated in
code. Sound effects are a mix: footsteps, impacts, the knife, breaking glass
and wood, explosions, UI clicks, reloads and the sci-fi guns are CC0
recordings from Kenney's audio packs (`assets/audio`); the rest is
synthesised. Weapons, zombies, cars, trees,
furniture, doors and castle pieces are public-domain (CC0) 3D models by
Quaternius, Kenney and Kay Lousberg. **[ASSETS.md](ASSETS.md) lists every
asset, its author, licence and download source**; `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.txt`
carries the notices that ship with the game. The UI fonts are Fredoka and Lilita One
(both SIL Open Font License).

## Play

Double-click **`Play.bat`**. It runs `build\SlopParty.exe` if it exists,
otherwise it runs the project from source with the bundled engine.

**On a Mac** (Apple Silicon or Intel, macOS 11+): download
`SlopParty-macOS.zip` from the GitHub release, unzip it and drag
**Slop Party.app** to Applications. The app isn't signed with a paid Apple
developer ID, so the first time **right-click it → Open → Open** (on newer
macOS: try to open it, then **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open
Anyway**). After that it opens normally. Settings are saved in
`~/Library/Application Support/Godot/app_userdata/Slop Party/`. On a Mac mini
with 4+ players, lower **Surface depth**, **Volumetric fog** or **Reflections**
in Options if it stutters.

**On Linux** (x86_64): download `SlopParty-Linux.tar.gz` from the GitHub
release, unpack it anywhere (`tar xzf SlopParty-Linux.tar.gz`) and run
`SlopParty/SlopParty.x86_64`. From a clone of the repo, run **`./play.sh`**
instead (it runs `build/SlopParty.x86_64` if it exists, otherwise the project
from source with the bundled engine; see *Restoring from GitHub*). It needs a
Vulkan driver (Mesa's RADV / ANV or NVIDIA's own), which every current
distribution ships.

**On a Steam Deck:**

1. In **Desktop Mode**, download `SlopParty-Linux.tar.gz` and unpack it, for
   example into `~/Games/SlopParty` (right-click it in Dolphin > *Extract >
   Extract archive here*).
2. Open Steam and choose **Games > Add a Non-Steam Game to My Library...** >
   **Browse...** > pick `SlopParty.x86_64` (set the file type filter to
   *All Files* if it isn't listed) > **Add Selected Programs**.
3. Go back to **Gaming Mode**: Slop Party is in your library under
   *Non-Steam*.

The Deck's own controls are player 1's gamepad, and any extra controllers
you pair (Bluetooth or USB) are players 2–6, so a Deck plugged into a TV
with a few pads is a full party. If the sticks move a mouse cursor instead
of playing, open the game's **Controller settings** in Steam and pick the
**Gamepad** layout. The first start on a Deck uses lighter graphics settings
(the Medium preset without ambient occlusion, bounce light, reflections,
surface depth or sun rays) and auto resolution, aimed at 60 FPS on its
1280 × 800 screen. Change them in Options like anywhere else. Settings are
saved beside `SlopParty.x86_64`.

In the lobby (a backyard party stage with a red carpet, velvet skirt and banners) each player presses **A** on their own gamepad to take a seat
(Space joins a keyboard + mouse player). Up/down picks what to customise
(animal, colour, hat, back) and left/right changes it.
Every match opens on a loading card with the mode, map, theme and a tip.

**The mouse works in every menu:** click a lobby chip to change it
(right-click goes back), click an empty plate to join as the keyboard player
or the X to leave, click the arrows on your plate to customise, click FIGHT
to start. In Options and the pause menu, hover and click; the wheel scrolls
and right-click closes.

**Controller not working?** The bottom-left corner of the lobby lists every
controller the game can see. If yours isn't there, the game can't see it:
pair it before starting the game, then restart. If it's listed as
"unmapped", press any button to join.

### If the game does not feel smooth

The game renders well above 60 FPS on a mid-range GPU, but it can only show
as many frames as the screen accepts. **Check the screen's refresh rate
first:** a 4K TV is often left at 30 Hz, which caps everything at 30 FPS
(the lobby shows a red warning when this is the case). In Windows:
Settings > System > Display > Advanced display > Choose a refresh rate >
60 Hz. If 60 Hz is not offered at 4K, use the TV's HDMI 2.0 port / cable,
switch on its "Input Signal Plus" (UHD colour) option for that port, or
pick 1080p or 1440p at 60 Hz.

### Options

Reachable from the lobby and from the pause menu, in pages (LB / RB or
Q / E to turn them):

- **Display:** window mode (windowed, fullscreen, exclusive fullscreen),
  **resolution** (Native, or 720p up to 4K, including ultrawide 21:9 and
  32:9 sizes; a size whose shape differs from your screen gets black bars),
  **HUD width** (ultrawide: keep health and ammo in a 16:9 area in the
  middle), V-Sync, frame rate limit. On a screen wider than 16:9 you see
  more to the sides (up to 125°), and two players split side by side
  instead of top and bottom. The default limit is
  **Display**: frames are paced by your screen's own refresh rate, which is
  the smoothest setting (a fixed 60 on a 120 or 144 Hz screen judders).
- **Graphics:** quality preset (Low to Ultra), anti-aliasing, motion blur,
  render scale (50-100 %, or 125 / 150 / 200 % **supersampling**: drawn
  bigger and shrunk, the smoothest image at a big GPU cost), **upscaling**
  (Bilinear, FSR 1 sharp, or **FSR 2**: temporal, so it also smooths the
  flicker and jagged texture detail, outlines and thin shapes that MSAA
  cannot reach; it replaces the anti-aliasing setting and works at 100 %
  too), FSR sharpening, shadows, ambient occlusion, surface depth, screen effects, reflections on wet ground, bounce light, light shafts in
  the air (Ultra; used with one or two views), bullet holes and scorch
  marks, bloom, scenery, soft focus, sun rays, and **auto resolution** (on
  by default): a busy moment lowers the 3D resolution briefly instead of
  dropping below 60 FPS. If the picture looks soft or blocky, try "On,
  never below 75%" or Off; high MSAA or supersampling makes it drop more.
- **Audio:** master, music and effects volume. Sounds are panned left and
  right relative to the nearest player's view.
- **Controls:** gamepad and mouse look speed, stick dead zone, a look speed
  for each player (Player 1-6, by join order), aim assist, rumble.
- **Gameplay:** field of view (a slider from 60 to 130 degrees, as on a
  16:9 screen), screen shake, view bob and sway, damage
  numbers, bot difficulty.
- **How to play:** every button, on foot, in a vehicle and in the lobby.
- **Modes:** what each game mode is about.

Options are saved in `slop_party_settings.cfg` beside the game executable
(on a Mac, or when that folder is read-only, in Godot's user data folder:
`~/.local/share/godot/app_userdata/Slop Party/` on Linux).

Up to six gamepads are supported; one of the six seats can be keyboard + mouse
instead. Screen layout: 1 player full screen, 2 stacked, 3–4 in a 2×2 grid,
5–6 in a 3×2 grid. Unused cells show a spectator camera and the scoreboard.

## Folder layout / portability

The whole game is this one folder. Copy it to a USB stick or another PC and it
runs; nothing is installed and nothing is written outside it.

```
Play.bat / Edit.bat   launchers (game / Godot editor), Windows
play.sh / edit.sh     the same for Linux / Steam Deck
project.godot         Godot project file
main.tscn             the only scene; everything else is built in code
scripts/              all game code (see docs/ARCHITECTURE.md)
assets/               3D models, plush animal meshes, audio (see ASSETS.md);
                      assets/voxel/ = the models baked into voxels
fonts/                Fredoka and Lilita One UI fonts + their licences
docs/                 architecture notes
tools/                build.bat / build.sh, setup_engine.ps1 / setup_engine.sh,
                      smoke.sh, balance.sh
engine/               bundled Godot editor + export templates  (not in git)
build/SlopParty.exe    standalone single-file game              (not in git)
build/SlopParty-macOS.zip  Mac app (Slop Party.app)             (not in git)
build/SlopParty.x86_64     Linux / Steam Deck single-file game  (not in git)
```

`build\SlopParty.exe` and `build/SlopParty.x86_64` on their own are also
fully portable: each is a single file with the game data embedded.

### Restoring from GitHub

`engine/` and `build/` are too large for git (each file is over GitHub's 100 MB
limit). After cloning:

```
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\setup_engine.ps1 -Templates
tools\build.bat
```

The first command re-downloads Godot 4.7.2 into `engine/`; the second
re-exports `build\SlopParty.exe`, `build\SlopParty.x86_64` (Linux / Steam
Deck) and `build\SlopParty-macOS.zip`. For the Linux release archive, run
`python tools\pack_linux.py build\SlopParty-Linux.tar.gz` afterwards (it
keeps the executable flag, which a Windows zip would lose).

On Linux (Steam Deck Desktop Mode included; needs `curl`, `unzip` and
`python3`):

```
tools/setup_engine.sh --templates
tools/build.sh
```

This downloads the Linux editor into `engine/` and exports all three builds
plus `build/SlopParty-Linux.tar.gz`. Without `--templates`, `./play.sh` and
`./edit.sh` still run the game and the editor from source.

A ready-made build is also attached to the repository's GitHub Releases.

## Developing

See [docs/ARCHITECTURE.md](docs/ARCHITECTURE.md) for how everything works and
how to add cards, change the map, or test without controllers.

Before every release, and after pulling anyone else's changes, run the smoke
test from Git Bash (or any shell on Linux). It plays a short bot match on every arena and in every
mode, each in a different theme, and fails on any script error or a run
below 50 FPS:

```
tools/smoke.sh          # all 13 runs, about 4 minutes
tools/smoke.sh quick    # 4 runs, about 1 minute
```

After adding or changing models, re-bake the voxel versions (about 2
minutes; the game would otherwise voxelize them while a match loads, once,
